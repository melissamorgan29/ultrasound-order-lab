# Ultrasound Order Lab — Final Project Rubric Alignment

## Project description
Ultrasound Order Lab is a scripted, browser-based sonography order-verification simulation. It uses fictional orders and requisitions, branching decision scenarios, immediate feedback, browser speech synthesis, practice scores, and paired readiness/follow-up checks. It is not a live AI agent or an automated clinical decision-support tool.

## 1. Selection of emerging technology
**Instructional problem:** Students may identify missing order information on a worksheet yet struggle to respond safely when an order conflicts with a facility worklist. A web-based interactive simulation lets students rehearse the entire verification, escalation, clarification, and handoff workflow without exposing patients to risk.

**Why this medium:** Realistic side-by-side documents, decisions with consequences, immediate coaching, and optional narrated guidance are difficult to reproduce in a static worksheet. Browser delivery supports repeatable practice without student accounts. This is an interactive simulation approach; verify that the instructor accepts it under the course definition of “emerging technology.”

## 2. Application of technology affordances
- Interactive term exploration and formative knowledge checks.
- Realistic fictional provider orders and requisitions for side-by-side review.
- Branching preceptor and phone-communication scenarios.
- Five-step integrated capstone: verify, escalate, clarify, recheck, handoff.
- Consequence-based feedback and mandatory guided recovery after unsafe choices.
- Automatic browser voice narration, replay/mute, and selectable device voices where available.
- Locally stored readiness and follow-up scores, individual feedback, and printable reports.

## 3. Pedagogical use
**Learning objectives:** Identify at least 4/5 critical order-verification concepts; locate discrepancies with at least 80% accuracy; make at least 4/5 correct first-choice capstone decisions. These are practice targets, not verified evidence of clinical competency.

**Learning design:** The experience progresses from terminology to detection, document comparison, correction, multi-case challenges, professional communication, and integrated decision-making. Authentic context aligns with situated learning; action-and-feedback cycles reflect experiential practice and formative assessment. Segmenting complex tasks into modules can help manage cognitive demands; optional narration and visible text provide multiple ways to access guidance.

**Assessment:** The readiness check establishes a baseline, and the same five-item follow-up check provides a simple within-learner comparison. Because items repeat and there is no controlled study, improved scores cannot establish causal learning gains. The capstone separately records first-choice performance; guided recovery does not increase the score.

## Suggested 5-minute demonstration
1. Show the homepage objectives and “Why this learning experience?” explanation.
2. Take the readiness check and point out that the score stays in the browser.
3. Show realistic order/requisition comparison and spoken preceptor guidance.
4. Open Clinical Capstone. Intentionally choose one unsafe response to demonstrate a consequence and recovery; then make a safe decision.
5. Finish the capstone and show the follow-up check and comparison.
6. Explain how this changes the learning task from recognizing errors to rehearsing a complete clinical workflow.

## Limitations / honesty statements
- Narration is generated by the student's browser, not a recorded professional voice. Automatic playback can be restricted by browsers.
- The branching simulation is scripted, not generative AI; no claims of adaptive AI tutoring should be made.
- Scores are saved locally, not collected centrally. Clearing browser data or switching devices may lose baseline scores.
- The five-item check is a learning aid, not a validated research instrument. The site does not establish clinical competency.
- No real patient identifiers should be entered; all cases are fictional.
- Order correction procedures and authorized roles vary by site and must follow facility policy.
